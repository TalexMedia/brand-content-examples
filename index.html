Brand + Creator · Garden & Outdoor
From Scratch: The Epic Gardening Way
  • The Home Depot and the gardening creator Kevin Espiritu turn a real property into a working garden and outdoor-living space over the course of the series.
  • Each episode takes on a separate stage of the build, including pathways, fencing, a greenhouse, an outdoor kitchen and the backyard pergola shown here.
  • Every episode is posted as a YouTube collaboration across six channels at once, so it appears in the feeds of all six audiences rather than only Home Depot's. Four smaller garden creators join Home Depot and Epic Gardening on the pergola build.
  • Epic Gardening brings 4.1 million subscribers to the partnership, against Home Depot's 659,000, so the creator's channel is the larger of the two.

Brand + Creator · Workspaces
Make My Space Work
  • The designer Alexandra Gater rebuilds the workspace of a real entrepreneur in each episode, working to a budget and around how that person actually runs their business.
  • Shopify Studios makes the series, and every episode follows the same path from what is not working, through the design plan and the build, to the reveal.
  • The series has its own channel, separate from both Shopify's and Alexandra's, which carries 26 videos and five million views in total. The episode here is the most watched of them.

Alexandra Gater is a home decor expert, editor, and stylist. In each episode, she helps entrepreneurs make their home offices, workspaces and storefronts more beautiful and functional on a budget.

Brand + Creator · Interiors & Outdoor
Mr. Kate
  • Kate Albrecht is a designer who runs her own home-makeover channel, with 3.7 million subscribers and 621 million views across the channel. The audience follows her rather than any brand she works with.
  • Brands fund the work in two ways. In her OMG We're Coming Over series she redoes another creator's room from top to bottom, with companies including Wayfair and Legrand supplying the products that go into the room.
  • She also films builds on her own property, which is what the video here is. She turns a corner of her backyard into a mud kitchen and play area for her kids, with a working faucet, a bridge and garden paths, over 23 minutes.
  • That backyard episode is sponsored by KiwiCo, a children's craft company, rather than by a furniture or building brand. The sponsor pays for the episode while the build itself stays the story.

Brand-Owned Series · Furniture
IKEA Home Tour
  • Five IKEA employees travel the country and make over rooms in real families' homes, working to whatever problem that particular household has.
  • The first season ran to 28 videos across 13 makeovers. IKEA's own figures put it at 5.6 million views, with store sales up an average of 4.1 percent during the weeks it aired.
  • It kept going well past that first season. The makeovers playlist now holds 245 videos with 59.5 million views between them, counting the full episodes alongside trailers and shorter tips.
  • The most watched episode is also the shortest. The teen bedroom episode runs three minutes against the ten to fifteen of a standard episode, and it has drawn 3.2 million views.

Brand-Owned Series · Beauty
Make Up Over Makeup
  • Two people with unresolved tension between them do each other's makeup while talking through what went wrong. It ran to three episodes.
  • The casting is the whole trick. Every pair had already fallen out in public before filming, so the audience arrives knowing the history and wanting to see how it goes. Doing someone's makeup also puts you close enough to touch them, which is a hard position from which to keep avoiding a conversation.
  • Episode one paired Chris Olsen and Ian Paget, TikTok creators who had documented their relationship for their followers and then split up.
  • Episode two paired Katie Maloney and Scheana Shay, castmates on Vanderpump Rules (the Bravo reality series set around Lisa Vanderpump's Los Angeles restaurants) who have fallen out and made up on camera since 2013.
  • Episode three paired Chelsea Blackwell and Jimmy Presnell from Love Is Blind (the Netflix series where couples get engaged before meeting in person). They got engaged in the pods and he refused to go through with the wedding, which made them one of the most talked-about couples of that season.
  • That third episode has done four times the numbers of any other, which lines up with it being the most recent and most public falling out of the three.

Honorable Mention · Brand Film · Furniture
Oddly IKEA
  • Twenty-five minutes of ASMR, all whispering and soft tapping, shot around IKEA bedding, storage boxes and a desk lamp. It was made as a back-to-college ad for dorm rooms.
  • No presenter, no story and no dialogue beyond the whispering, and it still holds 3.5 million views with 7,774 comments. It is the one on this page that took the biggest swing on format.
